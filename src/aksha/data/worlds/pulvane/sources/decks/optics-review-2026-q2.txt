ENG-117: Perfusion-adaptive LED drive
Nikhil Barua, optics engineer, and Rafael Domingo, firmware lead
Pulvane | 9 June 2026
[speaker notes]
(none)

--- page 2 ---
Why this matters
- On cold fingers and small fingers the green pulse signal gets weak and the ring misses beats
- On darker skin the green channel loses more light, so the same LED current gives a weaker pulse
- A missed beat looks like an irregular interval, so a weak signal produces false rhythm notices
[speaker notes]
This is the main source of the false notices we still see after the motion gate.

--- page 3 ---
Method
a) Measure the perfusion index on the green channel every 10 seconds.
b) If the perfusion index stays below 0.4% for 3 readings in a row, raise the green LED current in 1 mA steps up to the current cap.
c) If the signal quality index is still below 0.6 at the cap, switch beat detection to the infrared channel.
[speaker notes]
Step c is what makes it work on the hardest fingers; the infrared channel is noisier but it does not fade the same way.

--- page 4 ---
Settings
Measurement interval    | 10 s
Perfusion trigger       | below 0.4% for 3 readings
LED step                | 1 mA
Current cap             | 8 mA
Signal quality floor    | 0.6
[speaker notes]
After the skin-temperature test on 2 June we agreed to cap the current at 6 mA, not 8; this page was not updated.
