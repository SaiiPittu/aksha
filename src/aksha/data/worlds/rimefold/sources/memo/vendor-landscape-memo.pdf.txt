Distribution: founders, ops leads

1. Why this memo

Rimefold runs refrigerated last-mile deliveries for independent pharmacies
and grocers in Ontario and Quebec. Today every van carries a paper chart
and a dial thermometer, and two pharmacy customers have asked for a
continuous temperature record per delivery before they renew. This memo
lists the monitoring vendors we have met or read about, what each one
sells, and what we know about price. It is a first pass, written from
calls, trade-show notes and the material vendors sent us; it is not a
recommendation.

A short note on the wider market. An industry survey of 210 carriers
(synthetic) found that 38% of them filed at least one temperature-excur-
sion claim in 2025. That figure is the survey's, not ours, and it covers
carriers of every size.

What we need, in short: a record for every delivery that a pharmacy will
accept, an alert to the driver when a tote drifts out of range, and some-
thing that copes with two temperature zones in one van (2 to 8 °C for
pharmacy, -18 °C for frozen grocery).

2. Glacivo TrackPoint (Glacivo Inc)

Glacivo sells a rechargeable cellular tracker that clips to the wall of
the cargo box. It reports every five minutes over LTE-M and sends an SMS
alert when a reading leaves the band set for that delivery. Their sales
lead quoted us CAD 22 per tracker per month on a month-to-month plan,
hardware included, during a call in August.

Their datasheet makes a strong accuracy claim; it is worth reading the
calibration appendix at the back before taking it at face value. The
tracker needs recharging roughly every five weeks.

3. Pellisar U8 (Pellisar Sensors Ltd)

Pellisar makes a single-use USB logger. You press start, drop it into the
tote, and at the end of the run the pharmacy plugs it into any computer
and gets a PDF report with the full trace. No app, no subscription. The
logger cannot alert anyone during the run; it only records. Pellisar is
the oldest name on this list and pharmacies know its reports. List price
is in their datasheet.

4. Coldhaven SafeTemp U8 (Coldhaven Supply)

Coldhaven is a newer brand that approached us at the Toronto cold-chain
trade show. They sell a single-use USB logger, the SafeTemp U8, in packs,
with free shipping across Canada. The reports look much like Pellisar's.
Coldhaven seems aimed at small fleets that buy loggers by the box. Their
price list is attached to this memo's folder.

5. Frostmark Reefer Link (Frostmark Telematics)

Frostmark is a telematics box that wires into the refrigeration unit
itself rather than sitting in the cargo area. It reads the unit's own
sensors (return air, discharge air, the target temperature) and the door
switch, and sends alarms through a web dashboard and a phone app. It
needs a technician to fit, about two hours per van.

Frostmark does not publish prices; every quote depends on the number of reefer units.

We asked for a quote for 60 vans in July and have not had one yet.

6. Varrow Gateway (Varrow Labs)

Varrow Labs sells a small gateway that sits in the cab and collects
readings from wired probes in the cargo box. There is no subscription:
the gateway pushes readings to a server you run yourself. Price is
CAD 349 per gateway, paid once, with probes extra.

The gateway's working range is printed in the brochure as -40 °F to 158 °F.

Varrow is a two-person company and support is by email only. Their
gateway would need our own engineering time to receive and store the
data, which we do not have this year.

7. Tundrel Halo (Tundrel Systems)

Tundrel sells BLE tags that sit inside each tote, and a gateway in the
van that relays the tag readings over LTE. This is the only option that
tracks each tote separately, which matters for mixed pharmacy and frozen
loads. Pricing is in the shortlist sheet that the ops team keeps; tags
and gateways are priced separately and in US dollars.

8. What we still need to find out

- Whether pharmacies will accept a report from a tracker rather than from
  a single-use logger. Two of our pharmacy customers named Pellisar's
  report format; neither has seen the others.
- What Frostmark charges.
- Whether Glacivo's accuracy holds at frozen temperatures; see its
  calibration appendix.
- How each vendor handles a van with two temperature zones.
